
Abstract
This report details a comprehensive Big Data analytics project undertaken for GlobalShop Inc., a fictional international e-commerce entity. The primary objective was to address critical business challenges in inventory management, customer segmentation, and marketing effectiveness by integrating and analyzing large-scale, disparate datasets. The project leveraged three distinct data sources: the UCI Online Retail II dataset, the Olist Brazilian E-Commerce dataset, and the RetailRocket E-Commerce dataset, totaling approximately 4.1 million records. The technical architecture employed Apache Spark for the Extract, Transform, and Load (ETL) pipeline, managing data cleaning, transformation, and harmonization. The processed data was modeled into a star schema and deployed in a SQL Server Analysis Services (SSAS) Tabular Model to ensure high-performance querying. Data analysis was conducted using T-SQL, and key insights were visualized through an interactive Power BI dashboard. Key findings include the identification of a significant Q4 seasonal sales peak (42% revenue increase), the critical contribution of a "High-Value" customer segment (22% of customers driving 68% of revenue), and the identification of the UK as the top-performing market (31% profitability). The project successfully demonstrates the tangible business value of a unified Big Data strategy, providing actionable recommendations for inventory optimization and targeted marketing initiatives.

1.0 Introduction
1.1 The Role of Big Data in E-Commerce
The 21st-century retail landscape has been irrevocably altered by the proliferation of digital technologies. E-commerce platforms generate an unprecedented amount of data, commonly referred to as "Big Data," characterized by the 'Three V's': Volume (terabytes to petabytes of data), Velocity (real-time streaming of clicks, transactions, and events), and Variety (structured data like transactions, semi-structured data like JSON logs, and unstructured data like product reviews).
For an e-commerce company like GlobalShop Inc., Big Data is not merely a technical challenge; it is a strategic asset. Harnessing this data enables organizations to move beyond simple historical reporting and unlock predictive and prescriptive insights. Effective Big Data analytics allows for deep personalization of the user experience, optimization of complex global supply chains, dynamic pricing adjustments, and highly targeted marketing campaigns. In essence, BigData facilitates the digital transformation of a retailer, enabling data-driven decision-making to become the central nervous system of the organization.
This project simulates a real-world implementation of a Big Data analytics solution designed to provide GlobalShop Inc. with this competitive advantage. By integrating disparate data sources, this project creates a "single source of truth" that empowers business leaders to understand what happened, why it happened, and what actions should be taken next.
1.2 Project Objectives and Goals
The overarching business problem for GlobalShop Inc. is a lack of cohesive insight derived from its fragmented data infrastructure. This results in inefficient inventory management (frequent stockouts of popular items and overstocking of others), low customer retention due to generic marketing, and an inability to accurately track key performance indicators (KPIs) across different regions and product categories.
Therefore, the primary objectives of this project are:
Data Integration: To design and execute an ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) pipeline using Apache Spark to integrate three large-scale, heterogeneous e-commerce datasets into a unified data warehouse.
Data Modeling: To develop a high-performance, scalable SSAS Tabular Model (Star Schema) that serves as the analytical backend for business intelligence.
Insight Generation: To perform in-depth SQL analysis to identify significant sales trends, profile product performance, and define clear customer segments.
Visualization: To build an interactive Power BI dashboard that communicates complex findings to executive stakeholders in an intuitive and actionable format.
1.3 Scope and Expected Outcomes
The scope of this project encompasses the end-to-end data pipeline, from raw data ingestion to the final executive dashboard. This includes:
Ingesting and profiling approximately 4.1 million records.
Cleaning and transforming data, including handling missing values, standardizing categorical data, and creating surrogate keys.
Building and deploying a data model (FactSales, DimCustomer, DimProduct, DimDate, DimRegion).
Defining and calculating core business metrics (e.g., Total Sales, Avg. Order Value, Profit Margin, Conversion Rate) using DAX.
The expected outcome of this project is a robust, production-ready analytics solution. This report will serve as a comprehensive chronicle of the project's methodology, presenting the simulated results, findings, and strategic recommendations as if they were derived from a live implementation. The final artifact is a BI solution that enables GlobalShop Inc. to optimize inventory, personalize customer marketing, and ultimately enhance profitability.2.0 Step 1 – Data Selection & Business Problem2.1 The Business Problem at GlobalShop Inc.GlobalShop Inc., despite its significant transaction volume, operates with a fragmented data architecture. Its different regional operations (Europe, South America) and functional departments (Sales, Marketing, Logistics) use siloed systems. This creates several critical business problems:Inventory Imbalance: The operations team cannot accurately forecast demand. This leads to costly overstocking of slow-moving items and, more damagingly, stockouts of high-demand products during peak seasons, resulting in lost revenue and customer dissatisfaction.Ineffective Marketing: The marketing team lacks a 360-degree view of the customer. Campaigns are generic ("batch and blast") and fail to differentiate between high-value loyal customers and first-time casual buyers. This results in wasted marketing spend and low conversion rates.Poor Strategic Visibility: Leadership cannot get timely, accurate answers to basic questions like, "What was our most profitable region last quarter?" or "Is our average delivery time improving?"This project directly addresses this problem by unifying the disparate data sources into a single analytical model.2.2 Data Source DescriptionsTo simulate a realistic global e-commerce environment, three distinct, publicly available datasets were selected, representing a combined ~4.1 million records.2.2.1 Online Retail II (UCI)Source: UCI Machine Learning RepositorySize & Scope: 1,067,371 transaction records from a UK-based online retailer (2009–2011).Structure: Single-table CSV. Key columns include InvoiceNo (transaction ID), StockCode (product ID), Description, Quantity, InvoiceDate, UnitPrice, CustomerID, and Country.Relevance: Provides a rich history of transactional data, crucial for product affinity analysis and tracking sales in the European market.2.2.2 Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset (Olist)Source: Olist (via Kaggle)Size & Scope: Approximately 300,000+ records across multiple tables (2016–2018).Structure: A relational schema including orders, order_items, customers, geolocation, and order_payments.Relevance: This dataset is vital for modeling the South American market. Its rich structure provides detailed information on logistics (shipping_limit_date, delivered_customer_date), payment methods, and customer geography (latitude/longitude).2.2.3 RetailRocket E-Commerce DatasetSource: RetailRocket (via Kaggle)Size & Scope: 2,757,163 behavioral event records over a 4.5-month period.Structure: A time-series log. Columns include timestamp, visitorid, itemid, and event (e.g., 'view', 'addtocart', 'transaction').Relevance: This dataset is critical for understanding the pre-transaction customer journey. It allows for the calculation of funnel metrics, such as conversion rates (views to cart, cart to purchase), which is impossible with transaction-only data.2.3 Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)An initial EDA was performed on the raw, ingested data to identify key characteristics and potential quality issues. These records are useful for overall revenue calculation but must be excluded from customer-specific analysis like segmentation or loyalty.Negative Quantities: The extreme negative values in Quantity (e.g., -80,995) represent transaction cancellations or returns. These were handled by moving them to a separate FactReturns table to avoid skewing sales revenue.Zero UnitPrice: Records with UnitPrice = 0 were flagged as promotional items or data errors. After analysis, they were excluded from revenue calculations to avoid deflating Avg. Order Value.Data Harmonization: The core challenge is that CustomerID (UCI), customer_id (Olist), and visitorid (RetailRocket) represent distinct business domains with differing grains. To ensure architectural integrity and prevent synthetic data corruption, the pipeline implements clean domain decoupling: UCI Online Retail forms the core Kimball FactSales star schema, RetailRocket behavioral clickstream is pivoted into FactFunnel, and the Brazilian Olist orders dataset is cleansed and staged as an independent auxiliary table (orders_clean.parquet) for logistics profiling, intentionally decoupled from the core retail FactSales model. A similar process was assumed for DimProduct.3.0 Step 2 – ETL (Extract, Transform, Load)3.1 The Apache Spark ETL PipelineTo process the 4.1 million heterogeneous records, a robust ETL pipeline was designed and executed using Apache Spark, simulated on a Databricks cluster environment. Spark's in-memory, distributed processing capabilities are essential for handling this volume and the computational complexity of the transformations.The conceptual flow of the pipeline is as follows:Figure 3.1: Conceptual ETL Flow DiagramData Sources (CSV/JSON on Blob Storage) → Apache Spark Cluster (ETL) → SQL Server (Staging) → SSAS Tabular Model → Power BIThe pipeline was executed in three main stages:Extract: Raw CSV and JSON files from the three data sources were loaded from a simulated Azure Blob Storage container into Spark DataFrames. Spark's schemaInference was used for initial loading, followed by explicit casting of data types (e.g., converting InvoiceDate string to a timestamp object, UnitPrice to DoubleType).Transform: This was the most intensive phase. Multiple transformations were chained together using the Spark DataFrame API. (See 3.2 for details).Load: The final, cleaned, and transformed DataFrames (representing the dimension and fact tables) were written via a JDBC connector to a staging database in SQL Server. This relational database acts as the persistent data warehouse layer, decoupling the Spark processing from the SSAS analytical model.3.2 Transformation and Harmonization LogicThe core of the project's data engineering work resided in the transformation stage. Key operations performed in Spark included:Cleaning and Filtering:Filtered out records where Quantity <= 0 or UnitPrice <= 0 from the main sales fact data (as identified in the EDA).Removed records with critical missing keys (e.g., InvoiceNo).Handled the missing CustomerIDs from the UCI dataset by assigning them a default CustomerKey of '-1' (Unknown Customer).Harmonization and Integration (MDM Simulation):DimCustomer: Customer dimension built from unique retail customer identities, deriving deterministic 64-bit SHA-256 surrogate keys (CustomerKey) and maintaining referential integrity to DimRegion. The Brazilian Olist customer records remain preserved in the decoupled auxiliary logistics domain (orders_clean.parquet).DimProduct: Created a unified product dimension. This was highly complex, requiring string normalization on the Description field (e.g., lower(), regexp_replace() to remove punctuation) to create a common product identity across datasets that lacked a shared SKU. A surrogate ProductKey was generated.DimDate: A comprehensive date dimension was generated using a Spark UDF (User-Defined Function) to populate fields like Day, Month, Quarter, Year, and DayOfWeek from the primary transaction date.Fact Table Creation:FactSales: The primary fact table was built from cleaned transactional retail records (UCI), joined with DimCustomer, DimProduct, DimDate, and DimRegion on deterministic 64-bit surrogate keys. In accordance with architectural best practices, the Brazilian Olist dataset is staged as an independent auxiliary table (orders_clean.parquet) for logistics profiling and delivery performance analysis, and is intentionally decoupled from the core retail FactSales transaction grain.FactFunnel: The RetailRocket data (event column) was pivoted using a groupBy('visitorid', 'itemid') and pivot('event') operation to create a table showing counts for view, addtocart, and transaction per user/item, which is used to calculate conversion rates.3.3 Data Retention and AuditingIt is crucial to track data loss during the ETL process to ensure integrity.
